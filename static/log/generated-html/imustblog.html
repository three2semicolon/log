why is it so difficult to post

it's been a while since i wrote or released a blog post, and i've been trying to identify what exactly it is stopping me.

i genuinely enjoy doing this, and i know that it helps me. everything i wrote in my welcome post about needing an avenue to get thoughts out remains true (and the thoughts are very much there). so why am I not writing them?

my immediate answer when asking myself this question was "life just got busy", which isn't false, but definitely isn't the whole truth. i've had the time and the opportunity, and have gotten a lot done over the summer (professionally and personally). at time of writing this i have 10 unfinished (or more accurately un-started) posts on my drive, various subjects and things that i want to speak on that i just haven't sat and fleshed out thoughts for. countless other ideas and topics that i think about and never write down.

me writing the blog post you're reading.

so, why not?

at the core of it, i believe its a failure to act intentionally and to give myself mental space.

as i write this i'm trying to identify the feelings i had throughout the summer that prevented me from doing what im doing now, realizing that it almost feels i've teleported from May to September. i've done a lot of walking, a lot of train rides, made some good food, did some traveling, plenty of lovely dates with my girlfriend, lots and lots of work. i remember being present, and what needed to be done was done (+ what needed to be enjoyed was enjoyed), yet none of my thoughts feel accessible.

view of the hudson river from poughkeepsie, a lot of time here waiting for my train.

what i think was missing was a decision to take the time and really think about what i'm doing and why i'm doing them, alongside how they make me feel. its far too easy to fill time via the infinite dopamine machine in my pocket or to focus on a song or a video, robbing myself of an opportunity to think. i watched many video essays, read up on plenty of new topics, and listened to lots of new music and radio shows in those times of emptiness, and while i do think it was productive, i also feel i was offloading my "thinking".

while i do want the process of posting to this platform to be spontaneous, it does require me to "think", consider my own thoughts, and feel my own feelings in ways that allow me to put it to words. i was autopiloting hard, blocking myself from writing.

i am not a writer

i've never planned to write or to be a writer. i've been historically "mid" at english and language arts, and have trouble expressing myself in words with my mouth, let alone on paper (or keyboard). i say this to say that writing is not easy or natural for me (yet), and i think the insecurities coming from not being good at something (publicly) were another major blocker.

i'm writing this post to remind myself that being "good" at this isn't the point. i don't need to research and understand everything in order to write. this is an artform of self: self-intimacy, self-respect, self-confidence, self-self. these are my thoughts, release them. document them.

i don't consider myself a "real" music producer, and yet i produce. similarly, i am not a writer, yet i will write.

i've been playing minecraft

last year i stumbled into this video, and learned about the concept of a forever world in minecraft:

in short, its a world that you play on forever. no expectations of a phase that'll end in a couple months, no rush to complete major in game objectives, no time limit, no urgency. just playing minecraft to create and to exist in it.

i think that much like blogging, building and creating in minecraft forces me to actively think about what i'm doing and consider the why. they both also give me low-risk opportunities to try and fail at things, something i'm learning to accept as important (kid-me would prefer to never fail at anything. adult-me hopes for the same often, but has seen thats not feasible).

but yeah, started a forever world last year and i'm having a lot of fun with it. slowly building a collection of buildings and areas, just now finding the end stronghold to "beat" the game after almost exactly a year. i've been playing minecraft since about 10 years old, and playing the game this way takes me back to those times. a lot of the love and joy i have for creation started back then, it's good to reconnect.

de-algorithming, de-googling, etc

in working to regain some agency over the past year, i grew increasingly frustrated with the tech world and the ways that these software companies treat their consumers.

social media apps/sites profit directly off of our attention, and they try their absolute hardest to keep it. they're designed specifically to maximize advertisement money, and any algorithm meant to show us what we "like" exists to make a CEO (and shareholders) more funds. there's a lack of care, and they're getting bolder about it.

with that, i've stopped using elon's twitter entirely, alongside tiktok. these were my two social media vices for a long time (~7 years, at least my entire college career). the algorithms knew how to keep me around, and there's a lot of FOMO knowing that content still exists. it was an addiction. a lot of addiction advice will tell you to ween away from your vice or to use it in moderation instead, but knowing myself i needed to drop it cold turkey and fully commit.

of course with there being so many apps/sites and so many companies that exist to make money, its hard to escape. i use instagram still, but utilizing Revanced to focus my usage on connecting with my friends or getting inspiration. trying to do the same to youtube (i hate shorts and reels).

still working on how to get my news lol. i think the solution is to find newsletters that i actually care about and subscribe to them. reddit works, but they're a bit too reddit-y on there for my tastes (i'm sure you know what i mean).

short-form content and rapid fire interactions have been successfully dumbing me down and distracting me for years. i'd like my time back and i'd like my tech back.

i switched off of google chrome so i can use an adblocker without needing a new workaround every other month (Vivaldi for now, i'd love to switch to helium if they get DRM). i've moved my note taking off of google keep and use obsidian tied with a mobile repo so i can always access and edit my notes. markdown files are just the best, i love them.

and finally i've completely changed how i want to consume my music. i've gone back to my old ways of downloading again, bringing back my old limewire habits. i'm building a full suite of python cli's that let me build and manage my new music library, and now hosting it via navidrome. this (+ symfonium on my phone) has effectively replaced music streaming services for me.

planning to use soundcloud, forums, and dj sets/mixes to discover my music. i'll only use spotify to serve this same purpose, instead of as a monthly bill. i specifically want to shoutout anysia kym's No ID Radio show, which has introduced me to a lot of great artists.

courtesy of the 02/14/2025 episode of the no id radio show

all of this to feel some sort of control again. we deserve for our tech to be our own, for our data to be protected, and to be respected as consumers.
